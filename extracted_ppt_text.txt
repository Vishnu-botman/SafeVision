=== Slide 2 ===
Problem Statement & Motivation
Problem statement
Existing camera-based safety systems detect workers, machinery, helmets and vests as separate objects
in individual frames. A helmet appearing in the frame does not prove that every worker is wearing one,
and a person near a machine is not automatically in danger. These systems often lack worker-level PPE
association, cabin-operator context and persistent proximity analysis across video frames.
▶PPE-only alerts ignore machinery motion and worker location.
▶A person visible inside a crane or excavator cabin may trigger a false proximity warning.
▶A brief zone crossing and prolonged exposure should not receive the same risk level.
▶Repeated false alarms reduce trust and can cause safety staff to ignore genuine warnings.
Project motivation
Convert frame-level detections into worker-specific, explainable safety events that use visual proximity,
machinery motion, PPE status and exposure duration.

=== Slide 3 ===
Who is affected?
▶Construction workers: face direct exposure to moving machinery and may also work without correctly
detected PPE.
▶Machine operators: can trigger false proximity alarms when a camera sees them inside an excavator, crane
or other cabin.
▶Site supervisors and safety officers: must inspect repeated alerts and can suffer alert fatigue when
warnings lack context.
▶Visitors and subcontractors: may enter restricted work areas without understanding local machinery
hazards.
▶Contractors and project owners: carry operational, legal and financial consequences when preventable
incidents occur.
▶Workers’ families and nearby communities: bear the long-term human impact of serious workplace
injuries and fatalities.
Direct user of the system
The immediate user is the site safety team, which receives worker-specific, explainable alerts instead of raw
detection boxes.

=== Slide 6 ===
Background / Problem Context
▶Published construction-safety work has established that modern object detectors can recognise workers,
helmets, vests and machinery.
▶Most benchmarks still score individual images using precision, recall and mean average precision.
▶A site-safety decision requires additional context: which PPE belongs to which person, whether machinery
is moving, and how long the worker remains exposed.
▶A visible person near a machine is not always unsafe. A confirmed operator inside a cabin must be treated
differently from an external pedestrian.
▶A short accidental detection should not create the same alert as a persistent worker–machinery interaction.
▶SafeVision therefore focuses on the transition from frame-level object detection to worker-specific temporal
risk events.

=== Slide 10 ===
Existing Limitations
▶Frame-only decisions: a single incorrect detection can create an immediate warning.
▶No worker-specific PPE relationship: detecting one helmet and two workers does not reveal who is
protected.
▶No operator context: a person inside a machinery cabin can be classified as an external worker in danger.
▶Static danger zones: fixed rectangles or circles do not respond to observed machinery motion.
▶No exposure measurement: brief entry and prolonged proximity receive the same treatment.
▶Weak cross-domain performance: PPE accuracy drops when lighting, camera viewpoint or workplace
type changes.
▶Incomplete datasets: selected datasets contain object boxes, but not complete worker–PPE–machine
risk-event annotations.
▶Unclear alert quality: many studies report mAP but omit false alerts per hour and event-level recall.

=== Slide 15 ===
Research Gap
▶Existing studies optimise detection metrics, but site deployment requires accurate safety-event decisions.
▶Most studies do not measure whether detected PPE belongs to the correct worker.
▶Frame-level methods cannot distinguish brief crossings from persistent exposure.
▶A nearby cabin operator and an external pedestrian may receive the same proximity warning.
▶Fixed image-space danger zones ignore observed machine movement.
▶Existing datasets do not directly contain complete worker–PPE–machine event annotations.
▶Cross-domain performance drops when models move between construction and manufacturing imagery.
▶Few studies report false alerts per hour, event recall or alert delay.
Gap addressed by this project
An integrated video pipeline that converts two independent detectors into worker-specific, operator-aware and
temporally stable risk events.

=== Slide 18 ===
Proposed Solution / Methodology
1
Prepare MOCS for worker and machinery detection. Harmonise SH17, CHV and SHWD for person/head,
helmet and safety-vest detection.
2
Train two specialised YOLO-style detectors from random initialisation and select checkpoints using
validation performance.
3
Run both detectors on every video frame and fuse their outputs without merging the two training label
spaces.
4
Use ByteTrack to maintain worker and machinery IDs, trajectories and exposure histories.
5
Associate helmet and vest detections with worker head and torso regions, then stabilise the result across
several frames.
6
Estimate machinery movement, classify cabin/external/uncertain worker context and construct adaptive
image-space danger zones.
7
Accumulate risk from zone occupancy, motion, PPE state and duration. Apply persistence and hysteresis
before creating an alert.
8
Produce annotated video, explainable alerts and a structured event log.

=== Slide 23 ===
Tracking, fusion and PPE association
1
Apply both detectors to the same frame.
2
Filter low-confidence and duplicate detections using class-specific thresholds and non-maximum suppression
where required.
3
Send worker and machinery boxes to ByteTrack, which assigns temporary IDs and matches them across
adjacent frames.
4
Assign each helmet to the worker whose upper-body region contains it with the strongest geometric
agreement.
5
Assign each vest to the worker whose torso region contains it.
6
Use temporal voting across several frames to reduce one-frame PPE errors caused by occlusion.
7
Mark competing or weak associations as uncertain; do not force every PPE item onto a worker.
Meaning of identity
Worker 01 and Worker 02 are temporary tracking IDs within one video. The system does not recognise real
people and does not need the same individuals to appear in the training datasets.

=== Slide 24 ===
Operator-aware adaptive temporal risk engine
For tracked worker i at time t, the prototype computes
Ri(t) = Gi(t) [wzZi(t) + wmMi(t) + wτTi(t) + wpPi(t)] ,
where Z represents danger-zone occupancy, M represents observed machinery motion or approach, T represents
exposure duration, and P represents PPE non-compliance.
▶Gi(t) applies operator context. A confirmed cabin operator suppresses the external-pedestrian proximity
alert.
▶An uncertain cabin state never receives automatic safe status.
▶The danger zone expands in the observed direction of machine movement.
▶Risk accumulates during continued exposure and decays after the worker returns to a safe state.
▶Hysteresis requires persistence before raising or clearing an alert.
Calibration
The weights and thresholds are engineering parameters, not legal safety distances. Tune them on annotated
validation videos and report their sensitivity.

=== Slide 25 ===
Novelty
Primary contribution
An operator-aware adaptive temporal risk engine that converts detections into worker-specific safety events.
▶Two from-scratch detectors specialised for scene objects and small PPE objects, followed by explicit
detection fusion.
▶Worker-specific PPE association instead of scene-level PPE counting.
▶Conservative separation of cabin operators, external workers and uncertain cases.
▶Motion-adaptive image-space danger zones rather than one fixed radius.
▶Exposure-time accumulation and temporal hysteresis to reduce unstable one-frame alerts.
▶Explainable event output containing the worker ID, machine ID, detected causes, exposure duration and
risk level.
▶Ablation against a fixed-zone, single-frame baseline to measure whether the proposed context actually
